How to Calculate BMR Correctly: The Mifflin-St Jeor Formula (With Example)

Last reviewed: August 2026 — By Ahmed Bream

BMR (Basal Metabolic Rate) is the number of calories your body burns at complete rest just to stay alive — breathing, heartbeat, brain function, temperature. It is the single most important number in many diet and calorie plans, yet most sites give you a bare number with no way to verify it. This article explains the correct formula, walks through a real example step by step, and shows the three mistakes that make BMR numbers wrong.

The Correct Formula (Mifflin-St Jeor, 1990)

The formula used by most reliable calculators is the Mifflin-St Jeor equation, published in the American Journal of Clinical Nutrition (Mifflin et al., 1990). It uses weight in kilograms (kg), height in centimeters (cm), and age in years:

Men:   BMR = 10 x weight(kg) + 6.25 x height(cm) - 5 x age + 5
Women: BMR = 10 x weight(kg) + 6.25 x height(cm) - 5 x age - 161

Source: Mifflin, M.D., et al., "A new predictive equation for resting energy expenditure in healthy adults", American Journal of Clinical Nutrition, 52(2), 1990.

Step-by-step worked example

Let us calculate BMR for a man, 80 kg, 180 cm, 30 years old:

  1. Weight part: 10 x 80 = 800
  2. Height part: 6.25 x 180 = 1125
  3. Age part: -5 x 30 = -150
  4. Man adjustment: +5
  5. Total: 800 + 1125 - 150 + 5 = 1780 kcal/day

A woman with the same body numbers uses -161 instead of +5: 800 + 1125 - 150 - 161 = 1614 kcal/day. The gender term is not a bias; it reflects that women on average have a lower lean body mass for the same total weight, which lowers resting energy expenditure.

3 common mistakes that make BMR numbers wrong

  • Using pounds and inches in the formula. Mifflin-St Jeor expects kg and cm. 1 kg = 2.2 lb and 1 cm = 2.54 inches. Plugging pounds and inches directly gives a nonsense result.
  • Forgetting age. BMR decreases slightly with age. A 30-year-old and a 60-year-old with the same body do NOT have the same BMR.
  • Confusing BMR with TDEE. BMR is energy at complete rest. TDEE adds digestion, daily movement, and exercise. They are different numbers — see our BMR vs TDEE guide.

When to use and when NOT to use BMR

Use it: to start a calorie plan, compare your resting burn across days, or understand your baseline before adding exercise.

Do NOT use it as medical advice. BMR formulas are population averages with an error of roughly +/-10 percent. They are not a diagnosis and cannot replace a doctor or dietitian. Our BMR Calculator is educational only.

FAQ

Can I calculate BMR without a calculator?

Yes. Use Mifflin-St Jeor: men 10xW(kg)+6.25xH(cm)-5xage+5, women -161. With 80 kg, 180 cm, 30 years, a man gets 1780 kcal/day.

Why is BMR different for women?

The -161 term accounts for lower average muscle mass at the same weight, taken from the original 1990 Mifflin-St Jeor study.

Why does my BMR change as I age?

The formula subtracts 5 x age. As age increases, resting metabolic rate tends to fall, so the recomputed BMR is a little lower.
